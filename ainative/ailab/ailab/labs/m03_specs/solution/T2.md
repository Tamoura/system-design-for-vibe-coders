# REMIT-342: let customers type what their family should receive

### Goal
A customer can type the amount their family should receive, in the family's currency, and Remit shows the quote they would pay for it: the QAR amount to send, the fee, the total and the rate, in the same shape as today's quote.

### Context
- `RemitService.quote(send_amount, receive_currency, ...)` in `src/remit/service.py` only goes forward: it takes the QAR amount to send. `fx_quote` in `src/remit/fx.py` turns QAR into the receive amount with the customer rate (the mid rate less the corridor margin, 4 decimals, half up) and rounds the receive amount half up to 0.01. A quote is valid for 60 seconds.
- Fees come from `src/remit/fees.py`. The tier is chosen by the whole send amount with the upper bound inclusive: for `STD-A`, 500.00 is in the first tier and 500.01 in the second.
- `validate_send` in `src/remit/corridors.py` checks the QAR amount against `min_send` and `max_send`, both inclusive (50.00 to 25,000.00 for `QAR-INR`).
- Hessa's notes (`lab-assets/hessa-notes.md`): customers type a round number; the quote must not change between the screen and the confirmation; she would rather the family got a little too much than a little too little.

### Behaviour
The customer rate for `QAR-INR` is 22.5720. For a typed target, the send amount is the smallest amount, in steps of 0.01 QAR, for which `fx_quote` gives the family at least the target. The fee is then priced on that send amount like any other quote.

| Family should receive | Send (QAR) | Family receives | Fee (QAR) | Total (QAR) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11,286.00 INR | 500.00 | 11,286.00 INR | 12.00 | 512.00 | exact; first fee tier |
| 11,286.01 INR | 500.01 | 11,286.23 INR | 12.50 | 512.51 | one hundredth of a rupee more crosses into the second tier; the fee rises by 0.50 |
| 10,000.00 INR | 443.03 | 10,000.07 INR | 10.86 | 453.89 | cannot be exact; 0.07 INR too much |
| 1,128.60 INR | 50.00 | 1,128.60 INR | 5.00 | 55.00 | exactly `min_send`; the minimum fee applies |
| 1,000.00 INR | 44.31 | none | none | none | refused: below `min_send` of 50.00 |

- Given the corridor `QAR-EGP` and a typed 6,501.00 EGP, then the send amount is 500.00 QAR and the fee is 15.50 QAR.
- Given a typed amount that needs more than 25,000.00 QAR on `QAR-INR`, then the quote is refused with the same error as a forward quote of that size.

### Acceptance criteria
- For `QAR-INR`, a typed 11,286.00 INR gives a send amount of 500.00 QAR, a fee of 12.00 QAR and a total of 512.00 QAR.
- A typed 11,286.01 INR gives 500.01 QAR, a receive amount of 11,286.23 INR and a fee of 12.50 QAR.
- A typed 10,000.00 INR gives 443.03 QAR and a receive amount of 10,000.07 INR; for every accepted target the receive amount is at least the target, and 0.01 QAR less would give less than the target.
- A typed 1,128.60 INR is accepted with 50.00 QAR; a typed 1,000.00 INR raises `AmountOutOfRange`, the error a forward quote of 44.31 QAR raises today.
- For `QAR-EGP`, a typed 6,501.00 EGP gives 500.00 QAR and a fee of 15.50 QAR.
- `RemitService.send(..., quote=quote)` accepts a reverse quote and stores `Transfer.send_amount == Money("500.00", "QAR")` for the first row of the table.
- `python -m remit quote QAR 10000 INR --receive` prints the same lines as `python -m remit quote QAR 443.03 INR`, and the forward command prints exactly what it printed before.

### Constraints
- Money is `Decimal` through `Money`; no floats. Rounding of the send amount is the one decision below; every other rounding stays half up.
- Reuse `fx_quote`, `quote_fee` and `validate_send` as they are. The reverse search must call them, not copy their arithmetic, so a later change to a rate or a fee cannot make the two directions disagree.
- The search is bounded: a first estimate and at most a few steps of 0.01 QAR.
- Change only `src/remit/fx.py`, `src/remit/service.py`, `src/remit/cli.py` and their tests. No new dependency.
- Money logic needs a second human reviewer (`CONTRIBUTING.md`).

### Non-goals
- No change to rates, margins, fee schedules or limits.
- No reverse quote from a currency other than QAR, and no screen: the command line only.
- No promise to hit the target exactly; the aim is at least the target.

### Decisions and open questions
- Currency: the typed number is in the receive currency of the corridor (INR, PHP, EGP or PKR), never in QAR; the customer always pays in QAR, and the quote shows both.
- Rounding direction: the send amount is rounded up to the next 0.01 QAR so that the family receives at least the typed amount. Rounding half up could pay the family less than they were promised; Hessa would rather the family got a little too much. The overshoot is shown on the quote. Finance is not asked yet, so Hessa confirms.
- Tier boundary: the fee tier follows the derived QAR send amount, as a whole amount with the upper bound inclusive, not the typed number. A target one hundredth above a tier edge can raise the fee (the second row of the table); that is how the schedule works today, and the quote shows it.
- Limits: `min_send` and `max_send` apply to the derived QAR amount, both inclusive, with the same `AmountOutOfRange` error. The KYC daily and monthly limits are still checked by `send`, on the derived send amount without the fee.
- Quote life: the rate and the 60 seconds come from `fx_quote` at the moment of the call; nothing changes there.
- Express: `--express` works as in the forward quote, with the surcharge added after the cap.

### Risks
- Rounding the wrong way either shortchanges the family or gives away money; the property test on the table of targets guards the direction.
- The fee can jump at a tier edge, which may puzzle a customer; support needs the sentence in Decisions.
- Two code paths for one calculation can drift apart; the constraint to call `fx_quote` removes that.
