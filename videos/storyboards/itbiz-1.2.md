**Pick 1 of 3 for this course.** The reveal is simple and visual: a small change took five months, but only about four weeks of that was work. A timeline of nine steps, with thin work blocks and long grey waits, shows it at a glance, and every total is recomputed from the lesson's map.

## Four weeks of work, five months of waiting

**Lesson:** 1.2 Mapping a value stream end to end: lead time, waits and hand-offs
**Why this lesson:** Everyone in the room blames a different step, and the records point to a wait nobody suspected. The map's totals (85 days waiting, 21.5 days working, flow efficiency about 20%) all check out against the lesson's table.
**Target viewer:** An IT manager or delivery lead who is asked why "small changes take a quarter" and has only opinions to answer with.
**Length:** about 85 seconds

- On screen: A board-room card with the question "Why does a small change to the cards app take a quarter?"

**2. 8-20 s - Everyone has an answer**
- On screen: Four speech bubbles pop up around the table: "Test environments are booked", "Requirements are incomplete", "The change board approves in one meeting", "Funding is quick once the paperwork is right".

- Visual/animation: Each step draws a grey "wait" bar, then a short blue "work" block. The grey bars are mostly much longer than the blue ones.
- On-screen text: "Nine steps · nine hand-offs · dates from the records"
- VO (EN): "Huda, the head of enterprise architecture, pulled the real dates for nine similar changes and brought one person from each step into the room. They laid out every step: how long it waited, and how long it was actually worked on."
- VO (AR): "سحبت هدى، رئيسة المعمارية المؤسسية، التواريخ الحقيقية لتسعة تغييرات متشابهة، وجمعت في الغرفة شخصًا من كل خطوة. ثم رسموا الخطوات كلها: كم انتظر العمل قبل كل خطوة، وكم استغرق العمل الفعلي فيها."

**4. 34-48 s - The totals**
- On screen: The blue blocks slide together into one short bar, "Work: 21.5 days". The grey bars slide into one long bar, "Waiting: 85 days". Together: "Lead time: about 106 working days".

- On-screen text: "21.5 days of work ÷ about 106 days of lead time ≈ 20%"
- VO (EN): "Add it up. Twenty-one and a half days of work. Eighty-five days of waiting. A lead time of about a hundred and six working days, so the change was being worked on only about a fifth of the time. And only about seventeen percent of items got through every step without being sent back."
- VO (AR): "لنجمع الأرقام: واحد وعشرون يومًا ونصف من العمل الفعلي، وخمسة وثمانون يومًا من الانتظار. زمن التسليم الكلي نحو مئة وستة أيام عمل، أي إن العمل الفعلي لم يتجاوز خُمس الوقت تقريبًا. ولم يعبر جميعَ الخطوات دون إعادة سوى نحو سبعة عشر في المئة من البنود."

**5. 48-60 s - The longest wait**
- On screen: A spotlight sweeps past build and test and stops on the longest grey bar: "Funding decision (quarterly): 30 days". Two smaller bars light up next: "Release window (monthly): 12 days" and "Demand review (monthly): 10 days". The change board bar stays dim: "3 days".

- On-screen text: "Longest wait: funding, 30 days. Not in engineering at all."
- VO (EN): "The longest wait was not in engineering at all. It was the quarterly funding decision: thirty days. Then the monthly release window, twelve, and the monthly demand review, ten. The change board Yousef came to defend was a wait of just three days."
- VO (AR): "لم يكن أطول انتظار في الهندسة أصلًا، بل في قرار التمويل ربع السنوي: ثلاثون يومًا. تلاه نافذة الإصدار الشهرية باثني عشر يومًا، ثم مراجعة الطلب الشهرية بعشرة أيام. أما مجلس التغيير الذي جاء يوسف ليدافع عنه، فلم يتجاوز انتظاره ثلاثة أيام."

**6. 60-72 s - Too much started**
- On screen: A funnel with 45 item tokens inside and about 2 dropping out each week.
- Visual/animation: The equation assembles: "45 items in progress ÷ about 2 finished a week ≈ 22 weeks".
- On-screen text: "Average lead time = work in progress ÷ throughput (Little's Law, 1961; a rule of thumb, not a forecast)"
- VO (EN): "There was a second clue. Forty-five items were in progress and about two finished a week. Little's Law says that means roughly twenty-two weeks for the average item. Starting less work shortens that, without hiring anyone."
- VO (AR): "وكان هناك دليل ثانٍ: خمسة وأربعون بندًا قيد العمل، ولا يكتمل منها إلا نحو بندين في الأسبوع. ووفق قانون ليتل يعني ذلك نحو اثنين وعشرين أسبوعًا للبند في المتوسط. والبدء بعمل أقل يقصّر هذه المدة دون توظيف أحد."

**7. 72-85 s - Closing card**
- On screen: The timeline again, with the three longest grey bars marked by sticky notes: "fund small changes monthly (Tariq)", "weekly release slot for low-risk changes (Yousef, with Sami)", "intake checklist (Reem)".
- Visual/animation: The funding bar shrinks with a dashed outline: "experiment, measure in two months".
- On-screen text: "Shorten the longest wait, not the busiest step."
- VO (EN): "They agreed three experiments, each aimed at a long wait, each with an owner and a measure. Shorten the longest wait, not the busiest step. Open lesson 1.2 and map your own stream."
- VO (AR): "اتفقوا على ثلاث تجارب، كل منها تستهدف انتظارًا طويلًا، ولكل منها مالك ومقياس. قصِّر أطول انتظار، لا أكثر الخطوات انشغالًا. افتح الدرس 1.2 وارسم مسار القيمة الخاص بك."

## Takeaway and call to action
Takeaway: shorten the longest wait, not the busiest step, and find it from real dates, not memories. CTA: open lesson 1.2 and build a current-state map from five to ten real items.
